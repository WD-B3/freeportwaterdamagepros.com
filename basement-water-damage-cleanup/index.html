Stay off wet stairs, keep away from the electrical panel if the floor is wet and shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source.

Basements take water in from several directions. Heavy rain can raise groundwater against the walls, a sump pump can fail, a floor drain can back up or a pipe can burst overhead. The source decides the plan, which is why the first call includes a few questions about what happened.

Basement emergency: first moves

Stay out if electricity is near

If outlets, a panel or appliances are in the water, do not enter. Call the electric utility or 911.

Find the source

A burst pipe shows as running water. A rain event shows as seepage at walls or floor cracks. Sewage backup shows as dark, foul water.

Check the sump pump

If it has failed, water may be rising. A pump with power but no discharge may be clogged. Do not stick your hand into a sump pit.

Raise belongings

Lift boxes, furniture and electronics off the floor to dry shelving or an upper level.

Freeport's terrain and sewer system

An EPA case study on Freeport describes a history of wet-weather sewer problems tied to the city's varied terrain, with separate sanitary and storm systems. Homes in low spots, or near a creek or river, can see more trouble when rain loads the system. Your own risk depends on your address.

On the city's east side along the Pecatonica River, a 2020 FEMA notice described a grant request to acquire about 150 flood-prone properties. If you live near the river, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurance agent about flood coverage.

Finished basements, contents and walls

A finished basement means carpet over concrete, drywall to the floor and sometimes built-in cabinets. Water wicks up drywall several inches above the visible line, so wall bases are checked with meters. Insulation behind the board holds moisture, and wet drywall near the floor is often cut out.

Carpet and pad are covered on our carpet water damage page, and wall repair is on wall and drywall water damage repair. Contents such as boxes and papers should be lifted off the floor early, since cardboard fails quickly.

Cleanup, drying and what we do not do

We extract the water with pumps and wet vacuums, remove affected materials and place air movers and dehumidifiers. Concrete takes time to dry, so readings are logged until it reaches normal levels. The drying standards are laid out on our structural drying page.

Restoration is not waterproofing. We dry and repair what the water damaged. If groundwater keeps entering, a waterproofing or drainage contractor needs to look at the cause. We say so plainly rather than suggest a repair we do not do.

Insurance for basement water

The Illinois Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage, and that sewer backup or sump pump failure may be covered only with an endorsement. Sudden pipe bursts may be covered. Photograph the water line on the walls, keep damaged materials until the insurer has inspected and avoid large permanent repairs until then.

Is a wet basement covered by homeowners insurance?

Sudden pipe failures may be covered. Flood is usually excluded, and sewer backup or sump failure may need an endorsement. Check your policy.

Will you waterproof my basement?

No. We dry and repair water damage. A waterproofing contractor handles prevention.

How do I know the walls are dry?

Moisture meters read the drywall and framing. Surface feel is not enough.
