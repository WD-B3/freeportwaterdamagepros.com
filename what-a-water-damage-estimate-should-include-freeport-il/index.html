What a Water Damage Estimate Should Include in Freeport, IL

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

A good estimate answers three questions: what is being done, where, and for how much. A poor one is a single number on a page. Because water damage jobs change as walls open and readings come in, a written estimate also needs a way to handle changes. Here is what a Freeport homeowner should expect to see.

The basic structure

An itemized estimate separates mitigation, which is the emergency work, from repair, which is the rebuild. Mitigation covers extraction, moisture mapping, removal of wet materials and drying equipment. Repair covers drywall, insulation, flooring, paint and trim. Many losses involve both, and the two are sometimes billed on different timelines.

What a written contract should say in Illinois

The Illinois Attorney General states that a written contract with all costs enumerated is required for home repair or remodeling work over $1,000. The contract should state the total cost, including parts, materials and any estimate charge, and give the contractor's full name, address and phone number.

It should also describe the work, list materials, put oral promises in writing, explain how change orders will be handled, give start and estimated completion dates and set the payment schedule. Written guarantees go in the contract if the contractor offers them.

Red flags in an estimate

  • A lump sum with no detail
  • No mention of moisture readings or a drying goal
  • A request to pay the whole job up front
  • Pressure to sign immediately
  • No explanation of what is included and what is not
  • A promise that insurance will approve the claim

The Attorney General warns against contractors who ask you to pay for the entire job up front. A small deposit, tied to stages of completed work, is a safer pattern.

Comparing two estimates

Put the two documents side by side and match them by room. Check whether both use the same water category, the same affected area and the same drying days. A big difference in price often comes from a difference in scope, not from one company charging more for the same work.

Ask each company to explain any line the other does not have. If one estimate leaves out moisture readings, that is not savings. It is missing work. See water extraction for what the first-day work normally includes.

Estimates in the villages

Distance and access can affect scheduling, but they should not change the nature of the written scope. A clear estimate for a home in Lena reads the same as one for a Freeport address. Ask how travel is handled and get the answer in writing.

Taxes, fees and disposal

Ask whether disposal of removed materials, equipment delivery and any administrative charges are included in the number or listed separately. Small line items add up. A clear estimate shows them in plain terms so you are not surprised on the final invoice.

Also ask what happens if the job ends early. Equipment billed by the day should stop when the structure is dry, not on a fixed schedule. That keeps the incentives aligned.

Look for a reference to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard in the drying section, since it is the published basis for water categories and drying goals.

Illinois rules that apply in Freeport

The Illinois Attorney General notes that before you sign a home repair or remodeling contract over $1,000, the contractor must give you the Home Repair: Know Your Consumer Rights pamphlet. If the sale is made and signed at your home, you have three business days to cancel.

That applies in Freeport and in the nearby villages. Ask for the pamphlet and the contract in writing before work starts, and keep copies with your insurance records.

What affects the price

Water category, the square footage affected, the materials involved, the number of drying days and the scope of the rebuild shape the total. A dishwasher leak in a single room costs far less than a pipe burst that wet three floors. We do not quote exact prices without seeing the loss, and you should be cautious about anyone who does.

The best test of an estimate is whether you could hand it to another company and get a comparable quote. If not, ask for more detail. For a burst line, our burst pipe water damage page shows the cost factors we use.

Common questions

Is there a charge for an estimate?

Practices vary. Ask whether there is a charge for the assessment and get it in writing. The contract should list any estimate charge in the total cost.

Will the first estimate be the final price?

Not always. Water jobs can change when walls open. The contract should explain how change orders are handled.

How long is a written contract required for in Illinois?

For home repair or remodeling work over $1,000, the Attorney General says a written contract is required.

What if I sign at home and change my mind?

The Illinois Attorney General says you have three business days to cancel if the sale is made and signed at your home.
