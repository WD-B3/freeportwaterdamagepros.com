Commercial emergency: what to do in the first hour

Isolate the water

Close the zone valve or the main shut-off for the affected area. Know where these valves are before you need them.

Kill power if safe

Shut off breakers to wet areas only if the panel is dry. Otherwise, call the electric utility.

Move inventory and records

Lift stock off the floor, and move paper files, laptops and servers away from the water.

Notify the right people

Tell the building owner or property manager, your insurer and, for tenants, your landlord.

Business water loss versus a house

A home loss is about one family. A commercial loss may involve tenants, shared walls, ceilings that serve neighbors and inventory that cannot be replaced overnight. The decisions are different, including who pays, which carrier is involved and how soon the space must reopen.

Freeport buildings and water history

Commercial properties near the river have seen high water before. In 2019, floodwater from the Pecatonica reached a UPS facility in Freeport, and in 2022 businesses along Van Buren Street north of the river were reported as directly affected. If your building is close to the Pecatonica or Yellow Creek, review its flood coverage with your insurance agent.

For outside flooding, our process follows flood damage cleanup, and sewage involvement follows sewage backup cleanup.

How we work a commercial loss

We start with safety and scope. Standing water is extracted with pumps and truck-mounted units. Wet ceiling tile, insulation and carpet are removed as needed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and moisture readings are logged.

Large buildings may be dried in zones so part of the space can reopen sooner. Readings in each zone show when that portion reaches dry standards. Process details are on our structural drying page.

Documentation for carriers and landlords

Business interruption and property claims depend on records. We provide a written scope listing rooms, materials, equipment and moisture readings. Photos taken before and after are included in the file. We do not negotiate with your carrier and we do not promise approval.

The Illinois Department of Insurance advises reporting a claim quickly and holding off on large permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Keep invoices for emergency steps.

What affects the cost of a commercial loss

Square footage

Building materials

Drying days and zones

Zoned drying may allow staged reopening.
