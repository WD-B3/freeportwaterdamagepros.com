What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Freeport

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

After water damage, the hardest decisions are about stuff. The couch, the mattress, the boxes in the basement and the cabinet full of dishes all look different the day after. A few rules, based on how contaminated the water was and what the item is made of, make these decisions easier.

Start with the water category

Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, water is grouped into three categories. Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line. Category 2 carries significant contamination, such as washer or dishwasher discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage and floodwater.

The category decides what is salvageable. Clean water gives you many options. Sewage and floodwater leave very few for porous items.

Items to treat as discard

Soft goods that absorbed floodwater or sewage are generally not worth keeping. That includes mattresses, pillows, stuffed toys and cushions. Food that touched the water and containers without a waterproof seal should go too. Cardboard boxes fail first, so move stored goods in plastic bins if you can.

If you are unsure about an item, take photos, set it aside outdoors or in a garage and ask your insurer whether to keep it for inspection. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises keeping damaged materials so your insurer can see them.

Items you can often save

Solid wood furniture with a durable finish, metal tools, glass, ceramic and many plastics can be cleaned and dried after clean water. Photos and papers can sometimes be saved by freezing them to buy time. Electronics need a separate decision, and the rule is to leave them unplugged until a technician has checked them.

A sorting method that works

Make three piles. The first pile is hard items that can be washed and dried. The second is porous items to photograph and discard. The third is anything you are unsure about. Keep the third pile outside the living area and ask your insurer or a restoration company before deciding.

Label each pile with a sticky note so helpers do not mix them. Work from the driest room to the wettest, and wear gloves and boots. Lifting wet furniture is heavy, so ask for help rather than risk a back injury.

Kitchen and pantry

Food that touched floodwater should be discarded, including canned goods whose seals are suspect. Dishes and pots can be washed and sanitized. Refrigerators that lost power need a separate check. For a leak that began under the sink, see appliance leak water damage.

Sentimental items

Some things matter more than their price. Quilts, wedding clothes and family furniture may be worth professional cleaning even when the odds are mixed. Ask a textile cleaner or furniture restorer, explain the water source and request an honest opinion before paying for treatment.

Photograph the item first and keep a record of what you decided. If an insurer asks later, you can show you tried to protect it.

What to do when handling contaminated items

Wear gloves, eye protection and boots when you handle anything that touched floodwater or sewage. Wash your hands afterward and keep children away from the pile. Do not mix cleaning products, since some combinations release harmful fumes.

Creek and river water around Freeport

The Pecatonica River and Yellow Creek both pass through Freeport. In 2022, reports described Yellow Creek rising and the Pecatonica reaching 15.4 feet against a flood stage of 13.0 feet. Water from either one is outside water, and it belongs in Category 3.

That matters for your decisions. Porous items that sat in creek or river water are generally discarded, while hard items can be cleaned and sanitized. Our flood damage cleanup page explains the structure side.

Cleaning what you keep

Wash hard items with hot water and detergent, rinse them and let them dry fully. For contaminated water, sanitize with a diluted household disinfectant according to its label. Do not mix cleaning products. Wear gloves and eye protection, and ventilate the room.

If you are dealing with belongings from a basement, the process for the room itself is covered under basement water damage cleanup. For neighbors in German Valley and the surrounding area, the same rules apply.

Photograph, then dispose

Take clear photos of each discarded item, with brand and approximate age where visible, before it goes in the trash. A simple list on your phone helps if you file a contents claim. Do not throw out large items until you have talked with your insurer.

Common questions

Can a mattress be saved after water damage?

Rarely. A soaked mattress holds water deep inside. Discard after floodwater or sewage, and consider discarding after any heavy soak.

Should I keep wet items until the adjuster comes?

If your insurer asks, yes, photograph them and keep them outside or in a garage. Ask first for large items.

Can I dry wet photos and documents?

Often yes if you act quickly. Lay them flat to dry, or freeze them to buy time. Do not peel stuck pages apart.

If Yellow Creek water entered my basement, can I keep the boxes?

Porous boxes and papers that touched creek water should usually be discarded. Hard plastic items can be cleaned and sanitized.
