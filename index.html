Insurance and flood coverage, explained simply

The Illinois Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, and that a National Flood Insurance Program policy usually takes effect about 30 days after purchase. Sudden pipe bursts may be covered, while ongoing leaks are typically excluded. Sewer backup often needs an endorsement.

We document the loss with readings and photos. Your insurer decides coverage. Read more in our flood damage cleanup guide.

Local knowledge

Why Freeport homes see water in more than one way

Freeport sits on the Pecatonica River, and Yellow Creek runs through town too. In March 2019 the river crested at a preliminary 17.26 feet, a level 2 emergency was declared and floodwater reached a UPS facility. In 2022, 7 to 12 inches of rain fell in 48 hours across parts of Stephenson County, the Pecatonica read 15.4 feet and Krape Park closed.

Not every loss comes from the river. An EPA case study describes separate sanitary and storm sewers in Freeport and a history of wet-weather sewer problems tied to varied terrain. The city also gets about 38 inches of precipitation and about 34 inches of snow in a normal year, so frozen lines and spring thaw matter as much as summer storms.

Neighborhoods across town have their own histories, from the Arcade Addition on the east side to the streets near Read Park, Taylor Park and Oakdale Nature Preserve. Highland Community College, the Stephenson County Historical Society Museum and the U.S. 20 corridor mark the places most people use to describe where they live. Whatever your address, the first hours decide how much needs to be replaced.

Water damage questions in Freeport

Do you answer emergency calls at night and on weekends?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24/7, every day of the year. Call (815) 314-9281 and describe the water source.

What should I do before a crew arrives?

Stop the water at the source if it is safe. Stay away from standing water near outlets, move valuables up, and take photos for your insurer.

Do you work with insurance companies?

We document your loss with moisture readings, photos and a written scope. Your insurer decides what is covered, and we do not promise claim outcomes.

Do you remove mold?

No. We handle water damage restoration. If you find visible mold, ask a mold contractor about the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard and Illinois licensing.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. The Illinois Department of Insurance says flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs a separate flood policy.
