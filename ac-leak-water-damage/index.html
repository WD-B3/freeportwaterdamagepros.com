AC leak water damage in Freeport, IL usually starts with condensation that has nowhere to go. An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as it cools it, and that water is meant to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, the pan overflows, and water appears in a closet, ceiling or hallway.

If you see water at the air handler, switch the system off at the thermostat. Call (815) 314-9281 if the water has reached drywall, carpet or flooring. The dispatch line is open 24/7, though many AC leaks are not emergencies if the water is small and contained.

What an AC leak looks like

A condensate backup often shows as a stain on the ceiling below an attic air handler, a wet closet floor where the furnace sits or a damp spot along a wall. Some leaks are slow and cause hardwood cupping or musty smells before any stain appears.

The cooling-season pattern

Northern Illinois has a real cooling season, and the AC runs hardest during humid summer weeks. That is when condensate production peaks and a partly blocked line can finally overflow. A leak found in July often means debris built up across several seasons.

Slow leaks matter. Water dripping onto insulation or a hardwood floor day after day can do more harm than a single obvious spill. For a ceiling affected from above, see ceiling water damage repair.

How we repair the damage

We stop the active leak by shutting down the unit, then check moisture in the ceiling, wall and flooring with meters and thermal imaging. Wet insulation and saturated drywall are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry what remains.

An HVAC technician needs to clear the drain line, fix the pan and check the system, so we do not do the AC repair itself. That separation means you may want both trades. Drying is verified with moisture readings, as outlined on our structural drying page.

Drying after an air conditioner leak follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which treats condensate water as a contained source but still calls for moisture readings before anything is closed up.

When wood floors are affected

A condensate line that backs up near a wood floor can cause cupping, where boards curl at the edges as the underside absorbs moisture. Callers often notice this days after the AC stops leaking. Drying is slow and controlled, and some floors flatten again once moisture equalizes.

We explain that process on our hardwood floor water damage repair page. Early moisture checks give a better outlook than waiting to see whether the floor improves by itself.

Insurance and AC leaks

Coverage depends on whether the leak was sudden or gradual. The Illinois Department of Insurance says maintenance-type problems such as ongoing leaks are typically excluded, while sudden damage may be covered. Ask your insurer, and keep photos, the date of discovery and the HVAC service record.

AC Leak Water Damage questions in Freeport

Is an AC leak an emergency?

It can be. A small drip into a pan is manageable after you switch the system off. Water reaching drywall, carpet or electrical equipment is urgent, and our line is open 24/7.

What causes an AC condensate line to clog?

Algae, dust and debris build up in the drain over time. A technician clears and flushes the line, and some systems have a float switch that shuts the unit off.

Can an AC leak damage hardwood floors?

Yes. Slow moisture can cup boards before you see a stain. A moisture reading tells you where things stand.

Do you repair the air conditioner?

No. We handle the water damage. Your HVAC technician handles the unit.
