Water Damage and Selling Your House in Freeport, IL: What to Disclose

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

If your Freeport home has had water in it, the question that follows is whether you must tell a buyer. In Illinois, sellers of residential property typically complete a disclosure form, and that form asks about water. This guide explains the idea in plain terms. It is general information and not legal advice, so talk to a real estate attorney about your own sale.

What the form asks about

The Illinois residential real property disclosure form includes questions about flooding or leakage, the basement or crawl space, and plumbing and other systems. A seller answers based on what they know, and the buyer receives the form before signing. The questions are written so that past problems and current ones both get reported.

The basic rule of thumb is to answer truthfully and completely. A water event that was fixed properly is a different story from one that was hidden. Records help you tell the true story.

A documented dry-out is the strongest answer a seller can give. Drying logs from a structural drying job show readings over several days. Pair them with the repair invoice and the buyer's inspector has something concrete to read.

Common questions from sellers

If the loss happened once and was repaired, say so and attach the records. If the basement has taken in water more than once, the disclosure should reflect that, along with what you did about it, such as a sump pump or regrading. Buyers tend to be more worried about a hidden pattern than a fixed event.

If you are not sure whether a past stain came from a one-time leak, do not guess. Ask your agent or attorney how to phrase an answer, and consider hiring an inspector or moisture check before you list. See basement water damage cleanup for what a proper dry-out involves.

Talking to buyers

Be calm and specific. Say what happened, when, how it was fixed and what records you have. Buyers generally respond better to a clear story with paperwork than to vague reassurance. If they bring an inspector, expect a moisture meter and a thermal camera, and be ready to show access to crawl spaces and basements.

If you live in Pearl City, where creek flooding has affected homes in past years, expect buyers to ask about flood insurance as well. Having your agent explain your coverage in advance speeds the conversation along.

If the sale is already under contract

New damage after you have signed needs prompt communication. Tell your agent, document the loss and talk to your attorney about the contract terms. Dry the home quickly so the buyer's inspection finds a clean, documented repair rather than a surprise.

A drying record that references the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and shows readings over several days is easier for a buyer's inspector to evaluate than a simple invoice.

Repairs before listing

  • Fix the source of any ongoing leak first
  • Dry and verify any damp areas with a meter
  • Replace stained or swollen materials rather than paint over them
  • Keep every receipt and photograph
  • Ask your insurer whether repairs affect any open claim

Painting over a stain without fixing the cause is the most common mistake. An inspector with a moisture meter will find it, and the discovery can hurt a deal more than the original leak would have.

Flood history and Freeport buyers

Buyers in Freeport often ask about the Pecatonica River, which has a flood stage of 13.0 feet at the Freeport gauge. In March 2019 it crested at a preliminary 17.26 feet, and in 2022 it read 15.4 feet after 7 to 12 inches of rain fell in parts of Stephenson County over 48 hours.

If your property is in or near a mapped flood area, be ready to answer questions about flood insurance as well. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows mapped areas by address, and a seller who has already checked looks organized, not evasive.

Insurance history matters too

Buyers and their lenders sometimes ask about claims history. Your own insurer holds those records. Ask your agent what a buyer can see and how to describe a closed claim accurately. See burst pipe water damage for the common cause behind many one-time claims.

If you are selling in Lena or Orangeville, the same disclosure habit applies. A short folder of dated records answers most buyer questions.

Common questions

Do I have to disclose water damage that was fixed?

The form asks about flooding and leakage, so talk to a real estate attorney about how to answer for your situation.

Will a drying log help a sale?

It can. It shows the area was dried to a goal and gives an inspector something to verify.

Should I hire an inspector before listing?

It is worth considering if you have had water. A moisture check can catch hidden damp areas before a buyer does.

Can I sell as-is after a flood?

Many sellers do, but the disclosure form still applies. Ask an attorney how it works for your sale.
