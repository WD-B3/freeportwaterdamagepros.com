This page covers the damage that follows a freeze. It is separate from a pressure failure with no ice involved, which is handled on our burst pipe water damage page. The difference is the cause, and the cause shapes what you do in the first hour.

Frozen pipe emergency: protect the house at thaw

Close the main valve

Shut off the water to the house before you attempt any thawing. A pipe that is split but frozen shut can begin to spray as soon as it warms.

Warm the area slowly

Raise the room temperature or aim a hair dryer at the pipe from the faucet end. Never use an open flame, torch or kerosene heater on a pipe or near wet materials.

Look for the split

Check along exterior walls, attic runs, crawl spaces and cabinets on outside walls. A bulge, frost on the pipe or a trickle at a faucet can point to the problem spot.

Call for drying early

If water has already reached floors or ceilings, call right away. Wet insulation in a cold wall can hold moisture for a long time.

Why freezing damage is different

Water expands by about 9 percent as it turns to ice. In a sealed pipe, that pressure can split the wall of the pipe, often along a seam, with the break forming where the ice blockage builds the most force. The split may sit quietly until the ice melts.

Because the break is often inside an exterior wall, an attic or a crawl space, the first sign can be a stain or a drip that appears hours after the weather warms. By then the water has usually been running inside a cavity, so moisture readings matter.

What a Freeport winter means for your plumbing

NOAA climate normals for the Freeport area show an average of about 34 inches of snowfall a year, so the cold season is long enough to stress exposed lines. The City of Freeport also posts guidance on preventing frozen pipes and meters, which is worth reading before the next freeze.

If a line freezes outside the house or at the meter, call the City of Freeport Water and Sewer Division at 815-233-0111, and use 815-233-0711 after hours. Ask them where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins. Inside the home, the cleanup is a private-property job.

How the cleanup works after a thaw

A crew finds the wet areas with meters and thermal imaging, extracts standing water and opens walls or ceilings where moisture is trapped. Insulation that got soaked is removed, since it dries slowly and keeps water against framing. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings reach dry standards.

Repair of the pipe belongs to a plumber. We coordinate so drying equipment is placed after the opened section is accessible and before it is closed back up.

Insurance and documentation after a freeze

Burst pipes that cause sudden damage may be covered, while ongoing leaks and neglect often are not. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises people to report quickly, stop the source of the water and hold off on large permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Photograph the split section and the wet rooms.

Keeping heat on in the house is also part of the picture, since many policies expect reasonable care. Ask your insurer what yours requires for vacant or seasonal homes. We provide a written scope listing rooms, materials and equipment.

What affects the cost of freeze damage

Break location

A split inside a wall costs more to access than one under an open sink.

Time before discovery

A drip over hours is smaller than a pipe that ran while the house was empty.

Insulation affected

Wet batting or blown insulation usually has to be removed.

Drying days

Cold, enclosed cavities can take longer to dry.

Rebuild scope

Opened drywall and trim may need replacing after drying.

Frozen Pipe Water Damage questions in Freeport

Do you handle frozen pipe water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Freeport?

Yes. Cold snaps do not wait for business hours, and the line is open 24/7. Same-day response is offered when available, and no arrival time is guaranteed.

Is it safe to thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Slow warmth, such as raising the room temperature or using a hair dryer, is generally safer than any flame. If you see water spraying, a bulge or a wet wall, shut off the main valve and call for help instead.

Do I need to dry out walls if the leak was small?

Often yes. Small leaks inside a cavity can keep insulation and framing wet for days. A moisture reading tells you whether the wall is dry.

Does homeowners insurance cover frozen pipes?

Sudden bursts from freezing are often covered, but coverage depends on your policy and on reasonable care. Check your policy or ask your insurer.
