Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill? How to Check for a Leak in Freeport

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

A water bill that doubles with no change in habits is a signal worth chasing. Most of the time the cause is a running toilet or a dripping line, but sometimes it is water escaping inside a wall or under a slab. This guide shows how to narrow it down in an afternoon, and what to do if the leak has already done damage.

Step one: read the meter

Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water. Find the meter, which is usually in the basement, a utility room or an outdoor pit. Note the reading, wait an hour without using any water, and read it again. If the numbers moved, water is going somewhere.

Many meters have a small low-flow indicator, often a triangle or dial, that spins when even a tiny flow passes. If it keeps turning with everything off, you have a leak somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Signs of a hidden leak

Look for stains on ceilings, warped baseboards, peeling paint, a warm spot on the floor, a constant sound of running water or a musty smell. A leak in a pressurized line can run for weeks before it shows on the surface. If the meter moves and you see none of these signs, a plumber with leak detection equipment can trace the line.

Leaks under a concrete floor are harder to spot and often show up first as a bill increase. Our slab leak water damage page explains the signs and what comes next.

A toilet test you can do in fifteen minutes

Lift the tank lid, add a few drops of food coloring and do not flush. Wait fifteen minutes. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and the toilet is quietly running. A new flapper is inexpensive and often fixes a surprising share of high bills.

If the toilet is fine, check for a sticking fill valve, a dripping shower arm and a leaking outdoor spigot. Make a list of what you checked so a plumber does not repeat it.

Keep the paperwork

Save the bill that spiked, the meter readings you took and the plumber's invoice. If you ask your utility about an adjustment, those records show when the leak started and when it was fixed.

What a leak does to a bill over time

A small steady leak can use thousands of gallons over a billing period. That is why a leak shows up in the bill before it shows up on the wall. Catching it early saves both the water and the material it would have soaked.

If you find the leak quickly and the structure is still dry, you may not need restoration at all. If it has already soaked something, see burst pipe water damage for the next steps.

Who to call about the bill

For addresses served by the City of Freeport, the Water and Sewer Division can be reached at 815-233-0111, and 815-233-0711 after hours. Ask about your meter reading, your billing history and whether the city offers any adjustment process for a leak that has been repaired. Policies differ by utility and can change.

Villages bill on their own systems. Lena can be reached at (815) 369-4016. In Pearl City, the village uses a flat-rate unmetered water system, so a bill jump there points to a rate change rather than a leak. The village office at 815-443-2141 can confirm.

Freeport wells and a large system

Freeport draws its water from municipal wells in the St. Peter Sandstone and deeper aquifers and serves about 9,500 connections. The treatment plant has operated since 1882.

A system that old and that large is a reminder that service lines vary widely in age and material. If your bill jumps and the meter proves a leak, repair it quickly, because a running line can also damage materials under the home.

If the leak already caused damage

Stop the water at the main or at the fixture valve. Photograph any damage and call your insurer. The Illinois Department of Insurance notes that ongoing leaks are typically excluded from standard policies, which is why dating the discovery matters. Report it as soon as you find it.

Then dry the area. Water that has been escaping for weeks may be in walls, insulation or subfloor. A crew can map it with a moisture meter and thermal camera, as described on our structural drying page.

For homes in Pearl City or German Valley, call the village water office first for meter details, then a plumber.

Common questions

How do I know if my meter shows a leak?

With all water off, a moving reading or spinning indicator means water is flowing somewhere.

Who do I call in Freeport about a high bill?

The City of Freeport Water and Sewer Division at 815-233-0111, and 815-233-0711 after hours.

Is a high bill covered by insurance?

Usually not by itself. Ongoing leaks are typically excluded. Ask your insurer about the damage the leak caused.

Why is my Pearl City bill higher?

Pearl City uses flat-rate unmetered water, so a change likely reflects the rate. Confirm with the village office.
