Water Damage Restoration in East Side, Freeport, IL

Water extraction, structural drying and written records for East Side homes east of the Pecatonica River.

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

Water damage restoration in the East Side of Freeport, IL starts with understanding where the neighborhood sits. An EPA community revitalization document describes the East Side as a neighborhood in the floodway of the Pecatonica River, and it notes that many families have lived there for generations. If water is in your home now, call (815) 314-9281 and tell us what happened.

Where the East Side sits and why water matters here

The EPA document names Stephenson Street as an entrance corridor into the neighborhood and places Taylor Park Elementary School nearby. It also names Currier Creek alongside the Pecatonica as a source of flooding. Exact boundaries are not defined by a city map, so we describe the area by these landmarks and not by a fixed border.

Residents quoted in that document describe recurring major and minor flood events, water entering at the ground floor and streets that floodwater can block. The document also says many areas lack working storm drains. Those are resident and planner descriptions, not a count of damaged homes, and we do not claim a number.

What the public record says about floodway rules

In December 2020, FEMA published a notice about a City of Freeport request to acquire and demolish about 150 flood-prone properties in the Pecatonica floodway in the Eastside community. Participation was voluntary, and the city grant page records FEMA clearing the project under NEPA on March 1, 2021. We do not know whether funding was awarded.

The EPA document also says floodway rules limit improvements to 50 percent of a structure's value before upgrades such as elevation are required. If your repair may be large, ask the City of Freeport which rules apply to your address before you commit to a rebuild. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, so check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Water damage we handle in East Side homes

  • River and creek water that reaches a ground floor, covered on our flood damage cleanup page
  • Standing water that needs pumping first, see water extraction
  • Wet basements and low rooms after heavy rain, see basement water damage cleanup
  • Sewage or drain backups after storms, see sewage backup cleanup
  • Wet framing and drywall that must be dried to a readable goal, see structural drying

Plumbing leaks also happen in any house, flooded or not. A failed supply line or water heater needs the same extraction and drying, and it is often covered differently by insurance than a river flood.

Safety steps before you go back in

Do not enter a flooded home or basement while power may be on. The City of Freeport has warned that electrical panels in flooded basements are a fatal hazard, and that floodwater can be fast-moving. Do not walk or drive through floodwater. Treat river, creek and sewer water as contaminated. Call 911 for any electrical or structural danger.

Insurance and documentation for a flood or a leak

The Illinois Department of Insurance says flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs a separate flood policy, which typically takes effect about 30 days after purchase. Sewer backup often needs an endorsement. Sudden pipe bursts may be covered, while ongoing leaks are typically excluded. Your insurer decides coverage.

Photos, dated notes and moisture readings help describe what happened. We record readings and write a scope of work you can hand to your insurer. We do not promise that a claim will be approved, and we do not adjust claims.

How a job usually runs

We confirm the water source and category, extract standing water, find hidden moisture with meters, remove materials that cannot be saved and dry the structure. Sewage and floodwater call for removal of porous materials, not just fans. We do not remove mold. If you find visible mold, ask a mold contractor about the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard and Illinois licensing.

Freeport water and sewer questions go to the City of Freeport Water and Sewer Division at 815-233-0111, or 815-233-0711 after hours. For the rest of the city, see our service area page, or start from the Freeport home page.

East Side water damage questions

Is the whole East Side in the Pecatonica floodway?

No source we found maps the whole neighborhood as floodway. The EPA document describes the East Side as located in the floodway, and a FEMA notice refers to flood-prone properties in the floodway. Check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Does a flood policy cover river water in my house?

A separate flood policy is the usual route, per the Illinois Department of Insurance. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood. Ask your agent about your own policy.

Can I start repairs right after the water drops?

Extraction and drying are reasonable first steps. Large permanent repairs may fall under floodway rules, so ask the City of Freeport first and talk to your insurer before rebuilding.

Is it safe to turn the power back on in a flooded basement?

No. Do not enter or restore power until a qualified electrician has checked it. The City of Freeport warns that electrical panels in flooded basements are a fatal hazard.
