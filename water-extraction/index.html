Emergency water extraction in Freeport, IL means removing standing water from floors, carpet and low rooms before it soaks into the structure. If water is pooling in your home right now, our line is open 24/7, so call (815) 314-9281 while you shut off the water source and keep everyone out of the wet area.

Extraction is the first physical step in a water loss. It is different from drying, which comes after. Pumps and extraction units pull out the liquid, and then air movers and dehumidifiers deal with the moisture left in wood, drywall and padding.

When standing water is an emergency

Water that sits on a floor for more than a day or two starts moving into subfloors, baseboards and the bottom of walls. Water that came from outside, from a sewer line, or from a failed appliance drain should be treated as unsafe to touch. While you wait for a crew, these steps keep the loss from growing.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the fixture shut-off. If you cannot find it, the City of Freeport water office can help with the curb stop at 815-233-0111, and 815-233-0711 after hours.

Power down only if it is safe

If the electrical panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, switch off circuits to the wet rooms. If the panel is wet or the floor is flooded, stay out and call the electric utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes, books, small furniture and anything on the floor onto a dry surface. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs that must stay on wet carpet.

Keep people and pets out

Standing water can hide hazards and may carry contamination. Keep children and animals out until a crew has checked the water category.

How much water is really on your floor

A single inch of water over a 1,000 square foot floor is about 623 gallons and roughly 5,200 pounds of weight. That is why a spill that looks small can still be a serious extraction job. The water also does not stay put. It wicks into drywall, soaks carpet pad and runs along joists to rooms you have not checked.

We measure depth and spread before choosing equipment. A crew uses moisture meters and a thermal camera to find wet areas that are not visible, such as the back side of a wall or the underside of a floor.

Pumps, wet vacuums and truck-mounted extraction

Equipment is matched to the depth and the surface. Submersible pumps clear deep water in a basement or low room. Portable extraction units work through carpet and pad. Wet vacuums finish hard floors and tight corners. For large volumes, a truck-mounted unit moves water quickly from several rooms at once.

What happens once the water is out

Removing the water does not finish the job. Materials that were under water still hold moisture, and the air in the room is loaded with it. The next phase is structural drying and dehumidification, where air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and dehumidifiers pull the vapor out of the room.

Readings are taken at the start and on later visits so you can see the numbers fall toward dry. If a material cannot dry in place, such as saturated pad or soaked insulation, it comes out.

Safety steps for standing water

Assume standing water can be energized if it touches outlets, cords or appliances. Do not wade into it until power to the area is off. Wear waterproof boots and gloves, and treat water from sewers, drains or outdoors as contaminated.

Creek and river water is a different problem

Freeport sits on the Pecatonica River and Yellow Creek. During the heavy rain of 2022, reports noted Yellow Creek rising in the city and several streets closed. Water that enters a home from a creek or river carries soil and bacteria, so it falls under Category 3 in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

That changes the plan. Porous materials that touched the water are usually removed instead of dried, and the work follows the steps on our flood damage cleanup page. If your water came from inside the house, such as a supply line, the path is usually simpler.

What affects the cost of extraction

Water category

Clean supply water is simpler than gray or black water, which needs protective gear and more removal.

Depth and area

Gallons removed and the number of rooms reached drive crew time and equipment.

Materials involved

Carpet and pad, hardwood, drywall and insulation each take different handling.

Drying days

After extraction, equipment stays running for several days and is checked on a schedule.

Rebuild scope

Anything removed, such as flooring or baseboard, may need replacement later.

Water Extraction questions in Freeport

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Freeport?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, including holidays. Call, describe the water, and follow the shut-off steps above while a crew is arranged. We do not promise an arrival time, but same-day response is offered when available.

Can I use a shop vacuum to remove the water myself?

For a small spill on a hard floor, yes, if the outlet and cord are dry and the water is clean. For carpet, deep water or anything near electrical equipment, use a professional crew, because moisture stays behind in pad and subfloor.

How long does extraction take?

Pulling the standing water is often a matter of hours once equipment is on site. Drying the structure afterward usually takes several days, depending on materials and how long they were wet.

Is extraction covered by insurance?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks or neglect often are not. Flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and call your insurer early.
