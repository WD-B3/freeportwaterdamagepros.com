How to Save Photos and Important Papers After Water Damage in Freeport

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

Wet photographs and papers are often the things people care about most. The good news is that many can be saved if they are handled within a day or two. The bad news is that time works against paper, because mold and sticking set in quickly, so the first hours matter.

First moves, in order

Do not rub or peel

Wet photos stick to glass and each other. Handle by the edges, and never peel apart pages that are stuck.

Clean water only

If the paper touched sewage or floodwater, handle with gloves and consider professional document recovery for items of high value.

Lay flat or freeze

Spread items on clean paper towels or blotting paper. If you cannot dry them within a day or two, put them in the freezer to stop damage.

Keep air moving

Use a fan across the room, not directly on the papers. Add a dehumidifier to pull moisture out of the air.

Photographs

Prints that are not stuck to glass can be rinsed gently in clean water to remove dirt, then laid face up on a clean surface to dry. Do not touch the emulsion. If prints are stuck to each other, soak them briefly in clean water until they separate on their own, never by force.

Digital photos are different. If a phone, camera or hard drive got wet, leave it unplugged and keep it dry. A technician may be able to recover data. See our note on electronics at the end of this guide.

Documents

Once dry, scan or photograph every important document and store the files in a cloud account. A second copy off site means the next leak is a smaller problem.

A small supply list

  • Clean paper towels or unprinted blotting paper
  • Wax paper to separate photos for freezing
  • Zip-top freezer bags
  • Clean trays or baking sheets
  • Gloves and a mask for anything that touched dirty water

Keep these in a labeled box with your go-bag. When water is spreading, you will not have time to look for them.

Where families store papers

Basements, attics and garages are the most common places for stored boxes, and also the most likely to get wet. If you can, move irreplaceable items to a closet on the main floor, inside a sealed plastic bin. For a wet basement, see our water extraction page for what the first hours look like.

Neighbors in Lena face the same storage choices. A shelf is better than the floor, and a bin is better than a box.

Scanning as you go

As each item dries, scan or photograph it right away. A phone camera in good light is fine for most papers. Name the files by date and content, such as deed or insurance policy, and store them in two places. Doing it while the paper is out and flat saves a second round of handling later.

Professional restoration works to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which includes guidance on contents. Archival items such as rare books and family archives often need a document recovery specialist instead.

Why off-site copies matter in Stephenson County

After the March 2019 flooding, Stephenson County emergency management said affected residents might need several weeks before they could go home. If you are away from a flooded house for weeks, you may not be able to reach papers stored there.

A cloud copy of your deed, insurance policy, identification and photos means you can file claims and rebuild records without waiting for access. The Pecatonica River at Freeport reached a preliminary 17.26 feet that year against a flood stage of 13.0 feet.

What to move first when you see water coming

If you have warning, move a small go-box to a high shelf or take it with you. Include identification, the insurance policy, the deed or lease, passports and a flash drive with copies. Put everything in a sealed waterproof bag.

When a loss has already happened in a basement, see basement water damage cleanup. If the paper is stored in an attic and a ceiling gave way, the process is in our ceiling water damage repair guide.

When to call a document recovery specialist

For rare books, family archives, irreplaceable photos or items that touched sewage, ask a document recovery or conservation specialist. They use vacuum freeze drying and other methods that homeowners cannot match. Ask about their process and cost first, and photograph the items before shipping.

Electronics, briefly

Leave wet laptops, drives and cameras unplugged. Do not turn them on to test them. Remove the battery if it is removable, dry the outside and take the device to a technician as soon as you can.

Common questions

Can I iron or microwave a wet document to dry it?

No. Heat can set stains and damage ink. Air dry it flat or freeze it.

How long can papers wait before freezing?

Within about 24 to 48 hours is best, because mold can begin in that window. Freezing buys time.

Do I need a specialist for family photos?

For prints that are stuck or very valuable, yes. Many ordinary prints can be dried at home.

What should I keep in a flood go-box in Freeport?

Identification, insurance policies, deeds or leases, a list of contacts and a flash drive with scanned copies, in a sealed waterproof bag.
