When water is spreading in German Valley

Water spreads quickly in a low-ceilinged cellar and slowly through the framing of an older farmhouse, so both matter. The first priority is the source. The second is getting standing water out before it wicks into the walls and floor above.

Dry out after extraction is described on our structural drying page. If the cause is a pump that stopped or a floor drain that backed up, the same page explains the readings that tell you when a wall is dry.

If water is still spreading, do this now

Turn off the supply

Close the main valve. On a private well, switch off the well pump at its breaker if it is safe to reach.

Mind the sump and the panel

Do not reach into a sump pit. If the panel is wet, call the electric utility from outside.

Lift and protect

Move boxes and electronics off the floor and set foil under furniture legs on wet carpet.

Call before cleanup

Photograph the damage first, then call so a crew can start drying.

Insurance, wells and documentation

The Illinois Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage, while sudden pipe bursts may be covered and sewer backup or sump pump failure may need an endorsement. Read your policy for those words and call your agent early. Report even when you are unsure about coverage.

Photograph each room, the water line and the failed part before cleanup. Keep damaged materials until your insurer has seen them. If a well pump, pressure tank or septic component was involved, note that on the claim and ask the relevant contractor for a written finding.

For outbuildings, garages and barns with concrete floors, drying is a slower process because concrete holds water. Moisture readings tell us when the slab is ready for stored items to return.
