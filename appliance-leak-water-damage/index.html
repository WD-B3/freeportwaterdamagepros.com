Appliance leaks often go unnoticed for hours. A washing machine can release a hose-full while the family is at work, and a refrigerator line behind the unit can drip for weeks. The result is a damp floor, swollen baseboards and a cabinet base that never dries.

Appliance leak emergency: what to do while you wait

Close the supply

Washer hot and cold valves, a dishwasher shut-off under the sink and a refrigerator valve behind the unit each stop the flow. If you cannot reach one, close the main water valve.

Mind the electrical cord

Do not reach into standing water to unplug a machine. Switch off the circuit if the panel is dry.

Pull the appliance out if safe

Moving it exposes the wall and floor behind it for drying. Take care not to strain your back or damage the supply line.

Start photographing

Take pictures of the leak source, model label and wet areas for your records.

Water category matters for appliances

Not all appliance water is clean. A refrigerator or ice-maker line carries potable water, while a dishwasher or washer drain can release gray water that contains soap, food or detergent residue. Under ANSI/IICRC S500, that difference changes how porous materials are handled.

Refrigerator or ice-maker supplyUsually Category 1

Clean supply water, but it can degrade if it sits in carpet or insulation.

Washer supply hoseUsually Category 1

Clean source. A drain or overflow can add contamination.

Dishwasher drain or overflowOften Category 2

Includes soap and food residue, so porous materials are treated more cautiously.

Where appliance water hides

Water follows gravity and gaps. It moves under cabinet toe-kicks, behind baseboards and into the subfloor below a laundry room. A leak on an upper floor can drop into the ceiling below. For kitchen-specific damage such as cabinet bases and sink areas, see kitchen water damage restoration.

How we handle an appliance loss

We extract standing water, then use moisture meters to map how far it spread. Wet baseboards, pad and drywall that cannot dry in place are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest, and readings are logged until the materials reach normal.

Appliance repair and replacement are separate trades. Once the water is controlled, an appliance technician or plumber should inspect the failed part. See structural drying for how drying is verified.

Insurance and who pays

Sudden appliance failures can be covered by a homeowners policy, though gradual leaks and maintenance issues are often excluded, according to the Illinois Department of Insurance. If you rent, the landlord's policy and your renters policy may each apply, so tell your landlord promptly in writing.

A washing machine hose burst while we were out. What now?

Close the water supply, unplug only if safe, and call. A washer hose can run for hours, so check the floor below and the walls around the laundry area.

How do I know if water got under my flooring?

Signs include raised or cupped edges, a musty smell, soft spots and baseboards that feel damp. A moisture meter gives a clear reading.

Will my renters insurance cover this?

Renters policies usually cover your belongings and sometimes living expenses, but not the building. Ask your insurer and tell your landlord in writing.
