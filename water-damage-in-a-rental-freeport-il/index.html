Water Damage in a Rental in Freeport, IL: What Tenants and Landlords Should Do

24/7 Emergency Line(815) 314-9281

By the Freeport Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

When water shows up in a rental, two people are involved and both have a stake. The tenant wants to be safe and to protect belongings. The landlord wants the building dried and repaired. A short set of written steps keeps that from turning into a dispute.

This is general information, not legal advice. For a lease question or a disagreement, talk to an attorney or a tenant resource.

Tenant steps, in order

Stop and stay safe

If you can safely reach a shut-off valve, close it. Stay out of water near outlets, and call 911 for any electrical hazard.

Tell the landlord in writing

Call if it is urgent, then send a text, email or letter with the date, location and photos. Keep a copy.

Photograph everything

Capture the source, the wet areas and damaged belongings, with dates and wide and close views.

Call your renters insurer

Renters policies often cover your belongings after a covered event, but not the building.

The Right to Repair Act

Illinois has a Tenant Repair and Deduct Act, often called the Right to Repair Act. In general terms, it lets a tenant arrange a repair costing the lesser of $500 or half of one month's rent after giving the landlord 14 days' written notice, and then deduct the cost from rent under the conditions the law sets. It has exceptions and conditions.

Do not treat that as a simple permission slip. Read the statute or get advice before you deduct anything, and keep receipts. The notice must be written, which is one more reason to put every leak report on paper.

Landlord steps

Respond fast. Shut off the source, call a plumber if needed and get a crew started on extraction and drying. The longer water sits, the more the damage spreads and the more problems follow, including odors. Notify your own insurer and keep a record of dates and tenant contact.

Give the tenant a written timeline and tell them what to expect, such as noise from drying equipment. Our water extraction page describes the first stage tenants will see.

What to put in your written notice

  • Your name, address and unit
  • The date and time you found the water
  • Where it is coming from, if you know
  • What you did to stop it
  • A request for repair and a timeline
  • Photos attached or described

Keep a copy and note how you sent it. Email and text leave a timestamp. If you also hand-deliver a letter, ask the landlord to sign a copy.

Staying healthy during repairs

If the unit smells musty, if the repair is taking long or if anyone in the household has breathing trouble, ask the landlord for a drying schedule and keep notes. Equipment noise and humidity are normal during drying, but the area should be getting drier each day, as explained in structural drying.

If the landlord does not respond

Keep sending written reminders and keep copies. Note each day the problem continues and any harm to your belongings or health. Then talk to a tenant resource or an attorney about your options under the lease and Illinois law. Do not stop paying rent before you have advice.

Whoever dries the unit should work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard and share the moisture log with the landlord and the tenant on request.

What a tenant's insurance covers

Renters insurance typically covers personal property after covered events such as a burst pipe, but flood is usually excluded. The Illinois Department of Insurance notes flood insurance is not typically included in homeowners and renters policies. A flood policy for contents can be purchased separately.

Keep a list of belongings with photos. Take it before a loss, not after. A short video walk-through of each room is enough to start.

Renters near the river

In Freeport, the Pecatonica River has a flood stage of 13.0 feet at the gauge, and in March 2019 it crested at a preliminary 17.26 feet. Tenants in low-lying buildings face the same exposure as owners, but they often have no say over the building's flood coverage.

If you rent near the river or Yellow Creek, ask the landlord whether the building has flood coverage, and consider a contents policy that includes flood.

Keeping the peace

Most rental water damage stories end well when both sides communicate in writing, move quickly and document the work. They go badly when a leak is reported by phone only, repairs drag on and nobody can show when anything happened.

For tenants in Lena and Orangeville, the process is the same. Our burst pipe water damage page covers one of the most common causes.

Common questions

Should I tell my landlord by phone or in writing?

Do both when urgent, but always follow up in writing so there is a dated record.

Does renters insurance cover the building?

No, it typically covers your belongings. The landlord's insurance covers the structure.

Can I withhold rent after a leak?

Not automatically. Get legal advice before withholding or deducting anything.

What if I must leave during repairs?

It depends on your lease and the situation. Keep receipts and ask your insurer and landlord in writing.
